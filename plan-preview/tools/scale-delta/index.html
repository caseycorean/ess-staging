Part Weight from Corner Scales

Weigh the car on four corner scales, remove a part (or install one), and weigh again. The difference tells you the part's weight — and how it splits between the axles tells you where it sat. No need to lift an engine onto a bathroom scale.

How it works

The part's weight is the change in the total. Its fore-aft location follows from how that change divides between the front and rear axles: if the rear scales lost 20% of it, the part sat 20% of the wheelbase behind the front axle. Side-to-side works the same way across the track.

Each scale reading is rounded, so the result has an uncertainty. This tool assumes each of the eight readings can be off by up to half a reading step and shows the worst case. That covers rounding only — it is not the scales' accuracy or repeatability, which you should check by weighing the car more than once. Small parts on big scales give large location uncertainty — use this method for heavy assemblies and a component scale for small parts.

Carry your measured weight and location into the Balance & CG Builder. See how ESS records measurements.
