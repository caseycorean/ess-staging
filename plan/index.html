Build planning

Plan your build.

Most builds go wrong before the first bolt is turned: parts get bought before the goal is clear. This guide walks from what you want the car to do, to a plan you can test — using the articles and calculators on this site along the way.

Save a baseline in My Garage as you work through the guide. Use a measured baseline when you have one, and mark the changes you still need to test.

1 · Name the goal

What should the car do better: turn in, put power down, stop, ride, or simply weigh less? Is it a street car, a track car or both? A clear goal is what lets you say no to parts that don't serve it.

  • Balance and weight placement: What 50/50 really means · Balance & CG Builder
  • Grip in corners and under braking: Understanding the traction circle · Load Transfer Calculator
  • Springs and ride: Spring Rate & Ride Frequency
  • Know what your parts weigh: How ESS measures parts · Part Weight from Corner Scales

2 · Record your baseline

Write down what you have now: total weight and corner weights if you can get the car on scales, wheelbase and track, tires, and the current suspension. Every later decision is measured against this.

Balance & CG Builder · Part Weight from Corner Scales · Research method

3 · Set your constraints

Budget, time, tools, the fabrication you can actually do, and what has to stay (comfort, registration, originality). Constraints aren't limits on a good build — they are design inputs that narrow the options to the ones that fit.

4 · Understand what drives the result

Before comparing parts, understand the mechanism you're trying to change: how tires share grip, how load moves when the car brakes and turns, and why where mass sits matters as well as the percentage on the scale.

Understanding the traction circle · What 50/50 really means

5 · Compare the options on the same terms

Put each option side by side: cost, weight and where it sits, fabrication required, what it changes and what it doesn't. Compare approaches before products. For example, a second-gen F-body owner improving the front suspension has four broad approaches:

6 · Model before you spend

Use the calculators for balance, load transfer and spring rates. Watch a change play out in a launch, a stop, a corner or a corner sequence, compare acceleration in Test, or explore front and rear suspension geometry before deciding what to measure next.

All calculators

7 · Build in stages, and measure each one

Make the reversible decisions first. After each stage, weigh, test and compare against your baseline — that is how you find out whether a change did what you expected, and it's how the ESS cars are developed too.
